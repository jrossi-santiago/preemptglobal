What It Actually Catches

GEOMETRY IS ONE LAYER. THE SET IS THE WHOLE DOCUMENT.

Clash detection is built to do one job extremely well: find two modeled elements occupying the same physical space, or violating a clearance rule, inside a federated model. When every trade has modeled to a coordination-ready level of development, it will find that duct-through-beam every time. That's not in dispute.

What it isn't built to catch: a general note on one sheet that prohibits what a detail on another sheet requires. A spec section that contradicts the drawing set it's paired with. A submittal missing a cut sheet. A fire-rated assembly called out in one place and dropped in another. A schedule that doesn't match the panel it describes. None of that is geometry — it's paper conflicting with paper, and a 3D model was never asked to check for it.

"If we're already running BIM coordination, why do we need this too?"

Because BIM coordination is scoped to the model, and the model is scoped to what's been drawn in 3D. A construction document review is scoped to the entire issued set — drawings, specs, schedules, and notes — whether or not it's modeled yet. Both are real and worth running. Neither replaces the other. Preempt Global runs alongside your BIM coordination process, not instead of it, and reports to the owner directly instead of surfacing findings inside a design-team coordination meeting.

What's Required To Even Run It

CLASH DETECTION NEEDS A FINISHED MODEL. WE JUST NEED THE ISSUED SET.

Clash detection is only as good as what's been modeled — and modeled to a level of development where a clash is even detectable. Early in design, on smaller trades, on renovation work, or on any team where one discipline is behind on modeling, there's no coordinated model yet to run the software against. Until then, the conflicts sit in the drawings and specs with nothing checking them.

Preempt Global doesn't require a model at all. Send the issued set — drawings and specs, in whatever format you already have them — and the review runs against what actually exists today, not against what would exist if every discipline were fully modeled and current.

Adoption, Addressed Honestly

IT'S THE INDUSTRY STANDARD. IT'S ALSO ONLY AS GOOD AS THE MODEL.

BIM clash detection is standard practice on complex projects for good reason — it catches geometric conflicts before anyone breaks ground, and any team running full BIM should keep running it. But "standard practice" and "complete coverage" aren't the same claim. It only sees what's been modeled to a coordination-ready level, by every discipline, kept current.

The written spec, the general notes, the schedules — the parts of the set that live outside the model entirely — none of that gets checked by a clash report, no matter how mature the BIM process is. Preempt Global reads the layer that sits underneath the model: the issued document itself.

The Owner-Side Objection

"ISN'T CLASH DETECTION ALREADY AN OWNER-SIDE CHECK?"

Usually not. BIM coordination is typically run by the design team's or GC's VDC group, scoped to keep the model buildable and the trades from literally colliding — a real and necessary function, but one that reports into the design/construction side of the table, the same way an architect's QC or a GC's coordination review does. Preempt Global is paid by the owner, reports to no one else, and runs alongside BIM coordination and your team's other QC/QA processes — not replacing any of them, and not answerable to them.

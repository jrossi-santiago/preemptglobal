Who Actually Does Your Review

A ROTATING TEAM OF 4–12 STRANGERS VS. ONE NAME YOU CAN CALL.

CHECKSET is upfront that reviews are "never performed by a single individual" — each project gets a team of 4 to 12 reviewers assigned based on relevant experience, managed by a lead reviewer who oversees the group. That's a real operational model, and for some clients the bench depth is the whole appeal.

But it also means the person explaining your findings on the post-review call isn't necessarily one of the people who actually reviewed your set — and the team composition can change project to project. Preempt caps capacity at four sets a month for the opposite reason: Joe Rossi, who spent 5+ years producing construction documents and running PM on jobs up to $60M, reviews personally and puts his name on every finding. Not a rotating bench. One accountable person.

Depth vs. single-point accountability — a real tradeoff, not a flaw on either side.

THEIR DASHBOARD IS IMPRESSIVE. IT'S ALSO HOMEWORK.

Credit where it's due: CHECKSET's deliverable is the most feature-rich in this space — interactive drawing mark-ups, discipline overlays, priority levels, a "Top 10 Critical Findings" list, and analytics dashboards, all hosted in a client web app. If your team wants to self-serve through a platform, it's a genuinely capable one.

Preempt takes a different bet: instead of handing you a dashboard to explore, every finding already comes ranked (High/Med/Low) with a dollar exposure and schedule impact attached — and a live walkthrough where Joe explains what actually matters, in order, out loud. Less software to learn. More decisions made in the room.

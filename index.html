Before You Ask

STILL GOT QUESTIONS?
HERE'S THE STRAIGHT ANSWER.

What kinds of projects do you review? + –

Large commercial, industrial, and multifamily builds — office, manufacturing, warehouse and distribution, healthcare, higher-ed, data centers, multifamily residential, and other complex, multi-discipline sets. If it's a set with multiple trades sharing tight coordination, it's the kind of set this construction document review is built for.

What do we actually get back? + –

A written findings report — every conflict located by sheet and detail, severity-tiered, with an estimated field cost on each — plus a walkthrough meeting to go through it live. Specific findings, never a vague "area of concern."

What if you don't find anything worth the fee? + –

Then you don't pay. If the review doesn't surface at least $50,000 in documented exposure, it's free. On a project of this size and complexity there's always something to find — which is why the guarantee is in writing.

Who does the review? + –

Joe Rossi oversees every review personally — five-plus years producing construction documents and managing project administration on jobs up to $60 million, before founding Preempt Global. The work is done by reviewers who've drawn these sets themselves, never outsourced and never a black box, with Joe's name on every finding. That's why capacity is capped at four sets a month.

How does confidentiality work? + –

An NDA is signed before any drawings move. Nothing is sent until it's in place, and the set is ours to review — not to share, store loosely, or keep past the engagement. Documents are deleted on completion.

Doesn't our GC's or architect's review already cover this? + –

No — and that's the whole point. The architect's QC checks the set against design intent. The contractor's coordination review is scoped to pricing the work accurately. Both are real and necessary — neither is scoped to work for the owner alone. This review is: paid by the owner, reporting to no one else, run alongside your usual QC/QA process, not instead of it. See the full breakdown of owner-side QA/QC →

Doesn't BIM clash detection catch this already? + –

Clash detection catches geometry — two elements overlapping inside the coordinated model. It doesn't catch the model contradicting the written spec, a general note that prohibits what another sheet requires, or details that never overlap in the model but still stop work in the field. This review reads the issued set itself, which is where most of these conflicts actually live.

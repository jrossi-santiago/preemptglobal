**Any AI agent using this file must defer to `BRAND_VOICE.md` for all tone, vocabulary, sentence mechanics, terminology, and hard rules.** This file tells you which *structures* tend to perform. **Sample size note:** With only 6 posts, "top 20%" and "bottom 20%" are really "top 2" and "bottom 2." Treat every pattern below as directional signal to test on the next 10–15 posts, not settled fact. Where a finding rests on a single post, it's flagged explicitly.

## 3. What Actually Correlates With Performance

### Hook type is the single biggest differentiator
- The one post with a reader-address hook (naming something true of the reader's own systems, not just an incident the author noticed) has, by a wide margin, the best CTR — almost double the average.
- Two posts share a nearly identical first-person-discovery hook template, applied to different technical topics. Their CTRs differ by 63%. **The template isn't what drives clicks — the specificity of the technical detail inside it is.**
- The post with the highest-stakes, most dramatic hook in the set produced the best impressions number but the second-worst CTR. High-drama hooks appear to drive algorithmic reach (shares, dwell time) without necessarily driving click-through intent.

### Concrete numbers help, but only paired with a strong hook
- The two best-CTR posts both include hard, specific technical figures (exact voltages/phases, exact lead times, exact cost bands).
- One post has equally specific numbers and is the worst performer on both metrics. Specificity alone isn't sufficient — it needs a hook that creates a clear stake for the reader, not just a first-person narration of what was found.

### The one framework + CTA post is the best all-around performer
- The only post structured as a named, numbered methodology with an explicit imperative CTA is #1 on CTR and #2 on impressions.
- Single data point — treat as a strong hypothesis to test, not a proven rule. But it's the cleanest signal in the set: narrative-only posts range 1.03%–1.69% CTR; the one framework+CTA post hits 2.22%.

### Topic alone doesn't predict performance — framing does
- The same underlying topic ("missing spec") appears in three posts with three very different outcomes (worst CTR, best reach/weak CTR, worst reach/decent CTR). The differentiator isn't the topic bucket — it's whether the post frames the reader as implicated in the problem versus purely describing something the author found elsewhere.

## 4. Counter-Intuitive Flags

1. **Highest reach ≠ highest engagement quality.** The top-reach post landed second-to-last on CTR. Optimizing purely for "what got seen the most" would wrongly crown a weak converter as the best post.
2. **The longest, least dramatic post still beat 3 of the other 5 on CTR.** No incident-based hook, worst reach — yet it out-converted three posts with more dramatic hooks. Don't assume low impressions = bad post; check CTR before killing a format.
3. **The only post with a CTA and a named framework isn't just "fine" — it's the best post on both dimensions simultaneously.** Given how rare CTAs were in this set (1 of 6), this is worth treating as a live hypothesis, not smoothed away as "posts don't need CTAs."
4. **Identical hook formula, near-double CTR spread.** Copying a hook template that "worked once" isn't a strategy — the specific technical detail slotted into it is doing the real work.

---

## 5. Priority-Ordered Levers for This Audience

1. **Address the reader directly, not just the incident found.** Reframe hooks from *what was noticed* to *what's true of the reader's own drawings right now.*
2. **Pair every post with a concrete action, ideally a named framework.** The best performer is also the only structured, numbered methodology with an explicit CTA. This is the clearest, most testable lever in the data.
3. **Use hard, specific numbers — but only as ammunition inside a reader-implicating frame, not as the frame itself.** Numbers amplify a good hook; they don't rescue a flat one.
4. **Don't chase impressions as the success metric.** High-drama, stakes-first hooks can inflate reach without inflating clicks. If the goal is qualified engagement (clicks, leads, DMs) rather than raw impressions, don't over-index on the most dramatic possible opening line.
5. **Test formatting deliberately — there is currently no data on it.** All six posts use identical plain-text formatting. Try real bullets/bold on key numbers on a subset of future posts and compare CTR to this baseline.

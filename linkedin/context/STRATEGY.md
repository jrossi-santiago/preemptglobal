---

**Operating principle:** We are not building an audience. We are writing for the ~200–2,000 people on earth who can wire us $8k–$25k this quarter. Every decision below optimizes for that buyer, and deliberately bores everyone else. Mass engagement is a vanity metric; the only number that matters is what touches the bank account.

**The core mechanic:** The sale closes silently, while we're asleep. A real buyer lurks for 3+ weeks, reads the archive at 2am, checks whether January-us says the same thing as July-us, and decides alone. Our job is to make that background check pass — not to persuade anyone live.

## Phase 0: Foundation (Week 1 — nothing publishes until this is done)

### 1. Profile as landing page
- **Headline:** who we help + outcome + timeframe + mechanism
- **About section:** their pain in their exact language → our mechanism → our proof → one flat DM CTA
- **Featured section:** best case study + one deep resource (proof, not lead-gen bait)
- **Custom button:** direct to booking page

### 2. Define the unfair advantage
Proprietary data, volume of client conversations, recognizable results, patterns nobody else has seen at our scale. **Filter rule:** if a competitor could have written the post, it doesn't publish.

### 3. Research sprint
Pull the top 10 creators targeting our exact buyer. Extract their highest-performing posts from the last 90 days. Catalog hook / structure / visual / proof / CTA into a swipe file. We reuse proven structures filled with our data — never invent formats for novelty.

### 4. Buyer resolution document
Write a one-page portrait of the buyer at "his Tuesday" resolution: what his inbox looks like at 4pm, the hire from March costing him $8k/month that he keeps out of guilt, the dashboard conversation where everyone said "it's seasonal" and nobody believed it. Every writer references this before drafting. If a post could apply to 40,000 people, it's rewritten until it applies to 200.

## Phase 1: The Content Engine

**Cadence:** Post daily. Mix: ~50% long-form deep-value, ~30% short-form high-signal, ~20% pure personality.

### Format 1 — Full-System Giveaways (the flagship, 1–2x/week)
- Give away the **entire** solution. Every step, the real numbers, the scripts, the exact order. No teasing, no "3 of 7 steps." Nobody can judge competence from a teaser — only from a finished thing sitting in front of them at no charge.
- Then show the terrain: the 40 hours it took, the 6 versions that failed first, the 4 fragile details that only make sense after being burned, the step that rots if not done inside 72 hours.
- **The giveaway is the credential. The difficulty is the pitch.** If it looks easy, he runs it himself. If it looks complete AND brutal to operate, he wires.

### Format 2 — Case Study Breakdowns (1–2x/week)
- Lead with a real result and real numbers. Walk through exactly how it was achieved, step by step.
- Our involvement appears late and naturally, as one step in the process — never as a pitch. The goal is installing belief. A man who watched us solve his problem in public doesn't need a case study; he watched it happen.

### Format 3 — Short-Form High-Signal Posts (2–3x/week)
- One sharp observation, pattern, or contrarian read only someone at our depth could make. Proprietary insight, not "consistency is key."

### Format 4 — Personality Posts (1–2x/week)
- Opinions, taste, attitude, how we think. This is how the lurker decides whether he *likes* us — and it closes more than proof does. Eight-figure buyers have been closed on attitude alone.

### Copy rules for every post
1. **Four-question test (cut every word that fails it):** Why me — why THIS reader and not a list. Why now — what in his world makes this the week it matters. Why you — our specific proof for a business like his. Why respond — the next step and why it's worth his time.
2. **Specific numbers in every hook.** Outcome + timeframe + mechanism. Real figures only.
3. **Write at Tuesday-resolution.** Describe his actual life back to him. Accept that 95% of readers feel nothing — that's the design. The one reader who thinks "if he can see my problem at that depth, he can see the fix at that depth" is the entire market.
4. **Visual proof where it exists** (dashboard, calendar, client message) — but proof serves depth, not scroll-stopping theatrics.
5. **No links in posts. No engagement bait. No "comment WORD" mechanics — ever.** A founder doing $300k/mo will not perform a trick in our replies for an audience. Comment-gating farms a list of non-buyers and disqualifies every real one.
6. **Archive consistency is a feature.** The lurker is screening for whether we contradict ourselves across months. Positions don't flip-flop for engagement.

## Phase 2: Conversion Mechanics (human-only, zero automation)

### The CTA
One flat line at the bottom of relevant posts:

> "If this is you, my DMs are open — not free."

This announces we're not there to chat, deletes everyone hunting a free consultation, and flatters the buyer into the category of men who pay for things.

### The DM
- **Never initiate. Never chase.** Chasing reads as hunger, and hunger reads as risk to a man with money. The account that never slides into DMs is the account he trusts.
- When he messages: no deck, no Calendly fired back in 4 seconds like a bot. One question — **"What's broken, and what did the last attempt cost you?"** — then silence while he types for 6 minutes uninterrupted.
- Sequence: qualifying question → relevant case study once he responds → pitch only after fit is confirmed → propose a specific time and date, never "let me know when you're free." 3–5 messages total.
- Slow, human response times. Most sales are lost by answering too fast and too eager.

### The Call
- He is pre-closed. He read the archive, decided alone, told his team our name, and booked. **Pitching un-sells him** — it reopens a decision he already made.
- Open: **"You've read my stuff, so you know what we do. Want the invoice, or do you have questions first?"**
- The number, once, flat, with a start date attached: **"$X, starts Monday."** No range. No "depends on scope." No nervous filler after the price.
- Then silence. The 3 seconds after a number is where amateurs negotiate against themselves.
- A buyer who decided alone defends the decision to his own team. A buyer who was talked into it looks for the exit before the wire clears.

### Deep resources (when we make them)
- One pain point, one resource, value extractable in under 20 minutes, framed as internal work product.
- Delivered personally in DM, never through an automated opt-in funnel. The resource builds trust; the human conversation books the call.
- Embed a case study matching the reader's exact situation. By the time he finishes it, he should feel we understand his business better than people he's paid.

## Phase 3: Distribution & Scale

- **Repurpose every LinkedIn post to X the same day** (<10 min): lowercase, hard line breaks, shorter sentences, more direct tone. One link in bio to the booking page — nothing in posts.
- **Repurpose winners every 6–8 weeks:** proven idea, better hook, updated proof, fresh examples. Most followers never saw v1; new lurkers definitely didn't. We're building a library of formats that already work.
- **Team accounts (after 60+ days of consistent inbound):** additional accounts run the same system with different angles and personal voices. The one thing that does NOT scale: automated DMs or delegated conversations with buyers. The human in the DM is the product.

## Measurement (weekly review)

Measure each post by its assigned job, never by likes:

| Post type | Metric |
|---|---|
| Full-system giveaways | Saves, profile visits, inbound DMs referencing it |
| Case studies | Profile visits, inbound DMs |
| Short-form | Impressions, follows from target-profile accounts |
| Personality | Follows, qualitative "right people engaging" |

**North-star funnel numbers:** inbound DMs from qualified buyers → calls booked → closes → revenue. Track cost-per-call and cost-per-close if paid amplification is ever used.

A post with 40 likes that produced one qualified DM beat a post with 4,000 likes that produced none. Views are what the platform pays us in; wires are what buyers pay us in. Only one of those is a KPI.

**Kill rule:** any format or pain-point angle that produces zero qualified DM conversations over 4–6 weeks gets cut. Double down on what generates conversations with real buyers, even if reach drops.

---

## What we explicitly do NOT do

- Comment-gated lead magnets or one-word CTAs
- Automated DM sequences or opt-in email capture funnels
- Instant replies, chasing, or follow-up pressure
- Teasers that withhold "the real sauce" for a call
- Pitching on calls to pre-closed buyers
- Optimizing for impressions, followers, or likes
- Writing anything a broke audience would love and a rich buyer would scroll past

The Core Difference

ONE REVIEW. ONE CLIENT.

LightTable states its platform supports architects, developers, and general contractors. That broad workflow is a strength — the software is built to help the whole project team surface issues, collaborate, track responsibility, and resolve findings across preconstruction.

Preempt Global is intentionally narrower. That doesn't make LightTable's platform work against the owner — it means the platform is built to support the whole project team, and Preempt is built to give the owner an independent point of view alongside that team.

Doesn't our project team already have QA/QC covered?

Your architect, consultants, contractor, and internal team should all be reviewing the documents — those reviews are necessary, and Preempt doesn't replace them. What it adds is a separate review focused on the gaps, conflicts, and ambiguities that can become financial or schedule exposure, run for the owner alone rather than as part of the design or construction team's internal workflow.

Collaboration is valuable. Independence is also.

AI Finds. People Decide.

FINDING AN ISSUE IS NOT THE SAME AS OWNING THE ANSWER.

LightTable's AI is built to identify coordination and constructability issues quickly, and its platform includes expert validation, issue tracking, comments, and workflow tools. That helps a project team process a large volume of information much faster.

But an owner still has a different question: which findings can materially affect my budget, schedule, or scope — and what should I push to resolve first?

Preempt's deliverable isn't a software queue. It's a focused owner-side report — findings ranked by priority, translated into estimated exposure and schedule impact, presented as a decision-ready list. The goal isn't more items to review. It's making the important ones harder to miss.

Repeatability vs. Independence

RUN IT AGAIN. THEN LET SOMEONE OUTSIDE THE WORKFLOW LOOK TOO.

LightTable's ability to rerun drawings, compare versions, track changes, and carry lessons forward from project to project is one of its strongest advantages. For teams managing many active projects, repeatable QA/QC can become part of the operating system.

Preempt serves a different purpose. We provide an independent review at the moments when the owner wants a separate assessment of the issued set — before permit, before GMP, before bid, before a major release, or when the project has accumulated enough complexity that another set of eyes is worth more than another internal workflow.

These aren't mutually exclusive. A project can run continuous software-assisted coordination and still benefit from an independent owner-side review before a major decision.

Automation scales the process. Independence tests the assumptions.

One More Objection

"IF AI CAN REVIEW THE SET, WHY HIRE A REVIEWER?"

AI can process large document sets quickly, compare information across disciplines, and surface patterns that would take a person much longer to find. That's valuable, and it's changing how construction QA/QC gets done. But AI output isn't the same as owner representation.

Someone still has to determine whether a finding is material, connect it to the project's contractual and financial exposure, understand the practical consequence of leaving it unresolved, and communicate it in a way that supports a decision. Preempt uses technology where it improves speed and coverage, but the engagement stays accountable to the owner. The final product isn't "the AI found this." It's "this is the exposure, this is why it matters, and this is what should be addressed now."

AI makes review faster. Accountability makes it useful.

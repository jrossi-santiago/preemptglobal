What "Owner-Side" Actually Changes

SAME WORD, "QA/QC." DIFFERENT CLIENT, DIFFERENT SCOPE.

Every large, multi-discipline project already has QA/QC happening on it — the design team runs it, the GC runs it, sometimes a code consultant runs it. Owner's QA/QC construction review isn't a fourth version of the same check. It's a review with a different client: paid for by the owner, reporting to no one else, scoped specifically to find the cross-discipline conflicts that turn into cost and schedule exposure for the party writing the checks.

That distinction is the whole point of an independent construction document review. Every other QA/QC pass on the set answers to someone with a different set of interests — necessary interests, just not the owner's specifically.

Why It's Different From Architect QC Or GC Review

TWO REAL REVIEWS ALREADY HAPPEN. NEITHER IS SCOPED FOR YOU.

This isn't a case against either one — both are real, necessary, and should keep happening exactly as they do today. The point is narrower: neither was ever built to answer to the owner specifically, so neither one closes the gap an owner-side review is scoped to close.

The Architect's QC

CHECKS THE SET AGAINST DESIGN INTENT

Did the mechanical sheet end up matching what the mechanical engineer meant to draw. Did the structural sheet match structural intent. That's a real and necessary check — it's just not the same question as "does this conflict expose the owner to cost or schedule risk."

The GC's Coordination Review

SCOPED TO PRICE THE WORK ACCURATELY

Run to keep trades from colliding in the field and to bid the set as drawn. Also real, also necessary — and also reporting into the construction side of the table, not the owner's.

"Doesn't our GC's or architect's review already cover this?"

No — and that's the whole point. Both are real and necessary. Neither is scoped to work for the owner alone. Owner's QA/QC construction review is paid by the owner, reports to no one else, and runs alongside your usual QC/QA process — not instead of it.

Before You Ask

STILL GOT QUESTIONS?
HERE'S THE STRAIGHT ANSWER.

What does "owner-side QA/QC" actually mean? + –

It means a construction QA QC review paid for by the owner, reporting to the owner alone, and scoped to protect the owner's interest specifically — cost exposure and schedule risk — rather than design intent or bid accuracy. It runs alongside the architect's QC and the GC's coordination review, not instead of either one.

Isn't the architect's QC the same thing as owner's QA/QC construction review? + –

No. The architect's QC checks the set against design intent — did the drawings end up matching what the design team meant to draw. Owner's QA/QC checks the set for cross-discipline conflicts that create cost and schedule exposure for the owner specifically, regardless of whether the set matches design intent.

Does the GC's coordination review cover owner-side QA/QC? + –

Not by itself. The GC's coordination review is scoped to price the work accurately and keep trades from colliding in the field. It's real and necessary, but it reports into the construction side of the table, the same way the architect's QC reports into the design side. Owner's QA/QC is the review that reports to no one but the owner.

What is a construction document QC process, step by step? + –

A demo/scoping call, an NDA signed before anything moves, the issued set (drawings and specs) sent over, and a 48-hour turnaround producing a written, severity-ranked findings report plus a live walkthrough meeting. No model or special software is required to start.

Who should order an independent construction document review? + –

The owner or the owner's rep, on any project with multiple trades sharing tight coordination — there's no minimum construction value. It's ordered specifically because neither the design team's QC nor the GC's coordination review is scoped to work for the owner alone.
